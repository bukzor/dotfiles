# `alert` log: warn on failure, bounded size

**Parent:** <https:../2026-11-18-000-alert-and-window-name-followups.md> (wave 1)

## Problem Statement

Two follow-ups on `bin/alert`'s `log_alert`, which appends one JSON line per
alert to `~/.local/state/alert.jsonl`:

1. It runs after the notification is out, under `set -e` and the ERR trap.
   A logging failure (jq missing, disk full, unwritable dir) makes a
   delivered alert exit non-zero. Make a logging failure warn on stderr and
   leave the exit status alone. Callers include LLM tool calls and
   backgrounded jobs, so the exit status is what they see.
2. The log grows without bound. Choose and implement a retention policy.
   The log's purpose is the audit in `PUSH_ATTEMPTS_LOGGED`: "what wanted me,
   from where, and did it reach me", asked after the fact. Prefer the
   simplest bounded scheme (e.g. trim to the last N lines when it passes a
   size) over a rotation daemon or logrotate config; it must stay safe when
   two alerts fire at once. State your choice and its numbers as a
   Decision; the user may veto.

Test both: a forced logging failure (e.g. `HOME` pointed at an unwritable
dir, or `jq` hidden from `PATH`) still exits 0 with a warning; the bound
holds after enough appends. Exercise the transports without spamming the
user: hide `powershell.exe` and `notify-send` from `PATH` so only the bell
and the hterm write run, and point `HOME`/the log at scratch, not the real
log.

Update the `ALERT_LOG_DESYNC` claim file to reflect the resolution (its
standing/frontmatter per the conventions of that claims kb; read a sibling
claim to match form).
