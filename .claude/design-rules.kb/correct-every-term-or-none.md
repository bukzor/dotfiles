# Correct every term, or none

A bias correction applied to one term of a comparison and not the other does not
make the decision more careful -- it moves the threshold. Discount a forecast
benefit and leave the forecast cost bare, and the gate is loosest for exactly the
options with the least evidence, which is the opposite of the intent.

Diagnostic: an estimate where one side is adjusted "for uncertainty" and the other
is a bare number. Ask what produced the bare one; if the answer is the same
forecaster who produced the adjusted one, it carries the same bias.

Two consequences:

- **The corrections compose.** A ⅔ discount on a predicted benefit plus a threefold
  inflation of a predicted cost means an option justified entirely by forecast
  needs a 9:1 story, not a 3:1 one. That is the intended severity, and it is
  invisible while only one term is corrected.
- **A correction is indexed to the estimator.** A coefficient fitted to one
  person's optimism, applied to another party's output -- an agent's, a vendor's
  -- is a number carried across a change of subject. Record who produced the
  estimate, or the correction has no referent.
