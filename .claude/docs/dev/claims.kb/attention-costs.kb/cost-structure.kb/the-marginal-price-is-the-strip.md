# Quote the strip, because the tip does not decide anything

When a token's cost comes up, the number that belongs in the sentence is
the strip — the write plus every replay it commits to — not the tip.

The tip is what an invoice attributes to the moment of entry, and it is
the right number for reconciling a bill. It is the wrong number for
deciding anything, because it prices two very different acts identically:
reading a large file at turn 1 of a fifty-turn task, and reading the same
file at turn 48. The strip separates them by a factor of three or four,
which is the whole content of the decision.

Stated as a habit: **a token's price is a function of when it enters, and
a quote that omits the remaining turns has omitted most of the price.**

This is a judgment about which of two correct numbers to say, so it is
signed rather than derived. What would overturn it is a change in
practice, not in fact — if context were priced once on entry, tip and
strip would coincide and the distinction would stop earning its words.
